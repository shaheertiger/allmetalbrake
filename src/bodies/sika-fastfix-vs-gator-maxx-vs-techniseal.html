Polymeric sand looks like a commodity — a 50-pound bag of fine sand with a binder in it — and the three brands people most often compare, Alliance Gator, Techniseal and Sika, sit side by side at the same stores. But the bags are not interchangeable. They are rated for different joint widths, they tolerate rain at very different points after watering, and they have different rules about temperature and drying before you walk or drive on them. Picking the wrong one for your joints is one of the most common reasons a paver job washes out or hazes over.

This comparison covers Alliance Gator Maxx G2, Techniseal RG+ and Sika FastFix. We did not install these sands ourselves. The figures below come from each manufacturer's published technical data sheets and product pages; where we could not confirm a figure, we say so. Formulas and sheets are revised regularly, so check the bag and the current data sheet before you buy. For a wider roundup that includes other brands, see our guide to the best polymeric sand for pavers.

⚡ Quick Answer: For most patios, walkways and driveways, Gator Maxx G2 is the most flexible choice: it fills joints from 1/8 to 4 inches, is rain-safe 15 minutes after watering, can be applied above 32°F, and carries a 15-year residential limited warranty. Techniseal RG+ is a solid general-purpose sand for narrow-to-medium joints up to 1 inch that resists unexpected rain 90 minutes after installation. Sika FastFix-110 covers 1/8 to 4 inches and is built for fast gel time, but its sheet asks for 24 hours dry before foot traffic and 72 hours before vehicles. In the UK and Ireland, "Sika FastFix All Weather" is a different, ready-mixed jointing compound.

The Short Answer

There is no single "best" polymeric sand; there is a best sand for your joints and your weather. The decision usually comes down to three questions.

How wide are your joints? If any joint is wider than 1 inch — natural stone, flagstone, bluestone, or tumbled pavers with irregular edges — Techniseal RG+ is outside its rated range, and Gator Maxx G2 or Sika FastFix-110 (both up to 4 inches) are the candidates. For standard concrete pavers with tight 1/8- to 3/8-inch joints, all three work.

How sure are you of the weather? Gator Maxx G2 is rated rain-safe 15 minutes after water activation in traditional installs. Techniseal RG+ says it can resist an unforeseen rain 90 minutes after installation but recommends planning for 24 hours without rain. Sika FastFix-110 claims a fast gel time to resist unexpected rain, and asks you to protect the surface from rain while it dries for at least 24 hours.

How cold will it get? Gator Maxx G2 can be installed above 32°F in dry conditions. Sika FastFix-110 lists a 40–95°F range for both air and surface. Techniseal RG+ says the temperature should stay above 32°F (0°C) during its 48-hour drying period.

Which "Sika FastFix"? Two Different Products

Search for "Sika FastFix" and you will find two quite different things, which causes real confusion.

Sika FastFix-110 is a polymeric sand sold in North America. Sika's product data sheet describes it as a high-performance polymeric sand for joints between interlocking pavers, stone, bricks and slabs, applied dry, compacted with a plate vibrator and activated with water — the same method as Gator and Techniseal. It is the product to compare directly with the other two, and it is the one we focus on below.

Sika FastFix All Weather is a ready-mixed, brush-in jointing compound sold mainly in the UK and Ireland. It is not sand you water in: Sika's literature says it can be applied in any weather, even in the rain, and tells you not to add extra sand. Its published minimums are a joint width of 5 mm and a depth of 20 mm, and in dry weather above about 15°C setting starts in 6–12 hours. It belongs to a different category — resin-type jointing compounds — and is not a like-for-like alternative to North American polymeric sand.

If you are in the US or Canada and the bag says "polymeric sand," you are looking at FastFix-110 or a similar product.

Joint Width: The Deciding Spec

Every polymeric sand is designed for a range of joint widths, and using it outside that range is the single most common cause of failure. Joints that are too narrow for the sand do not fill to full depth; joints that are too wide do not bind strongly and crack or wash out.

Techniseal RG+ is rated from 1/16 inch (1.5 mm) to 1 inch (2.5 cm), with a minimum depth of 1-1/2 inches. Its data sheet also tells you when to choose something else: for high-humidity areas, false joints, joints wider than 4 inches, heavy traffic such as large commercial lots, steep slopes, or surfaces exposed to standing water, Techniseal recommends its HP2 high-performance sand instead. That is useful honesty — RG+ is a general-purpose product, not an everything product.

Gator Maxx G2 is rated from 1/8 inch to 4 inches in traditional installs, and up to 2 inches for overlays (pavers laid over an existing concrete slab), with a minimum depth of 1-1/2 inches. Alliance lists it for concrete, natural stone, wet-cast and clay pavers, and for patios, walkways, pool decks, driveways, parking areas, streetscapes and steep slopes. For driveways it requires fully interlocked pavers.

Sika FastFix-110 is rated from 3 mm to 100 mm — 1/8 to 4 inches — for joints between interlocking pavers, stone, bricks and slabs, on residential and commercial projects including walkways, patios, driveways, pool surrounds, parking areas and light-traffic roadways.

Measure your widest joints before buying. For natural stone such as bluestone or flagstone, where joints often vary from narrow to well over an inch, a 1/8-to-4-inch product is the safer choice. For machine-made concrete pavers with spacer bars, all three are in range.

Rain, Temperature and Cure Time

Rain is the biggest risk in any polymeric sand job. Water activates the binder; too much water too soon, before the binder has gelled, washes it out of the joint or onto the paver faces, where it dries as haze. Our guide to how to use polymeric sand covers the watering technique, and the products differ mainly in how quickly they stop being vulnerable.

Gator Maxx G2 is the most rain-tolerant on paper: Alliance says it is rain-safe 15 minutes after water activation in traditional installs (24 hours for overlays), credited to what it calls Rapid Set technology, and requires only 15 minutes of dry weather after activation. It can be installed above 32°F in dry conditions.

Techniseal RG+ says it starts to set a few minutes after wetting and becomes water-resistant 90 minutes after installation, so it can survive an unforeseen shower. For best results, Techniseal still recommends planning for 24 hours without rain, and says the temperature should stay above 0°C (32°F) during the 48-hour drying period. The surface must be dry before you spread the sand, or the polymer will activate early and stick to the pavers.

Sika FastFix-110 claims a fast gel time to resist unexpected rain after installation, lists an application range of 40–95°F for both air and surface, and says to let the surface dry for at least 24 hours — protected from rain — before foot traffic, and 72 hours before vehicles.

Whatever you choose, read the forecast for the full drying period, not just the first hour. A downpour on uncured sand is the most common reason people end up searching for how to remove polymeric sand haze.

Haze, Dust and Ease of Installation

Polymeric haze is a thin film of binder left on paver faces when sand residue is wetted. All three manufacturers address it, in different ways.

Gator Maxx G2 is marketed on ease: "no dust, haze free, no blower, one watering." Alliance says its "intelligent polymers" neutralize haze and dust, and that the sand is ASTM C144 graded to fit tight joints. If you are a first-time installer worried about haze, that is a meaningful claim — though basic good practice (sweep and remove residue, water gently) still applies.

Techniseal RG+ takes the traditional route: spread on a dry surface, sweep into the joints, compact with a plate vibrator (using a rubber mat on textured pavers), repeat until joints are packed to the bottom of the chamfer or at least 1/8 inch below the top, and remove all sand residue before wetting. The compaction step is critical: Techniseal says that without mechanical compaction, joints will remain weak and fail quickly.

Sika FastFix-110 follows a similar method: sweep in, compact with a plate in different directions, repeat, level with a leaf blower and blow off excess, soak sections of about 50 square feet for roughly 60 seconds from about 3 feet away, starting at the bottom of any slope, then blow excess water off the surface. Sika says the second blowing step is necessary to remove any residue left on the pavers.

None of these is difficult, but each manufacturer's method is slightly different, and the warranty usually depends on following it. Use the instructions for the bag you bought rather than a generic video.

Price Points and Warranties

One common question is why polymeric sands differ so much in price. Within these three, the higher price usually buys a wider joint range, faster rain resistance, easier haze-free installation, or a longer warranty — not "stronger" joints in a simple sense. A product rated for 4-inch joints uses a binder system designed to hold a wide, deep joint together; a product rated to 1 inch can be simpler and cheaper.

Warranties differ too. Alliance offers a 15-year limited warranty on Gator Maxx G2 for residential projects (1 year for commercial and institutional work), with the remedy being a refund of the purchase price, proof of purchase required, and installation to Alliance's specifications and industry standards. It excludes moss, mold and mildew. Check Techniseal's and Sika's current terms for RG+ and FastFix-110; we did not find comparable published residential warranty terms on the sheets we read. As with any warranty, it typically covers the material, not your labor.

Which One for Your Project?

Natural stone, bluestone or flagstone with wide or irregular joints: Gator Maxx G2 or Sika FastFix-110. Techniseal RG+ tops out at 1 inch.

Standard concrete paver patio or walkway with tight joints: any of the three. Techniseal RG+ is often the most economical; Gator Maxx G2 is the most forgiving to install.

Paver driveway: all three list driveways. Gator requires fully interlocked pavers; Sika says to wait 72 hours before vehicles. Make sure the base and edge restraints are sound first.

Pool deck or an area you pressure-wash often: Gator Maxx G2 lists pool decks specifically, and all three are designed to resist erosion once cured. Avoid aiming a pressure washer straight into the joints, whichever you use.

Unsettled weather or late-season job: Gator Maxx G2, for its 15-minute rain-safe rating and 32°F minimum.

High humidity, standing water or steep slopes: Gator Maxx G2 lists steep slopes; Techniseal recommends its HP2 rather than RG+ for these conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the best polymeric sand: Sika FastFix, Gator Maxx or Techniseal RG+?

For most projects, Gator Maxx G2, because it covers joints from 1/8 to 4 inches, is rain-safe 15 minutes after watering and installs above 32°F. Techniseal RG+ is a good choice for joints up to 1 inch. Sika FastFix-110 matches Gator's joint range but needs 24 hours dry before foot traffic and 72 before vehicles.

What is the difference between brands of polymeric sand at different price points?

Mostly joint range, rain tolerance, ease of installation and warranty. Premium sands tend to handle wider joints, resist rain sooner after watering and claim haze-free installation; cheaper sands may be limited to narrower joints and need longer dry windows. Match the sand to your joint width first, then to your weather.

Is Sika FastFix All Weather the same as polymeric sand?

No. Sika FastFix All Weather, sold mainly in the UK and Ireland, is a ready-mixed jointing compound you brush in, and Sika says it can be applied even in the rain. Sika FastFix-110, sold in North America, is a polymeric sand you sweep in, compact and water.

Which polymeric sand is best for bluestone?

Bluestone joints are often wider and more irregular than concrete paver joints, so choose a sand rated for wide joints — Gator Maxx G2 or Sika FastFix-110 (both up to 4 inches). Check the minimum depth (1-1/2 inches for Gator) and test a small area for color and haze first.

Is there a new type of polymeric sand on the market?

Manufacturers update formulas regularly — Alliance's "G2" generation and Techniseal's "NextGel" line are examples — and the main improvements are faster rain resistance, lower dust and haze, and wider joint ranges. Resin-type jointing compounds such as Sika FastFix All Weather are a separate category that is more common in Europe.

How long does polymeric sand take to cure?

It varies by product. Gator Maxx G2 is rain-safe 15 minutes after activation; Techniseal RG+ resists rain after 90 minutes and has a 48-hour drying period; Sika FastFix-110 needs 24 hours before foot traffic and 72 before vehicles. Cool, humid weather lengthens all of them.
