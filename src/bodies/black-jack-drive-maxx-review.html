Black Jack Drive-Maxx is one of the most widely stocked driveway sealer ranges in North America, and the three numbers on the pails — 500, 700 and 1000 — generate a steady stream of questions: what is the difference between them, is the 1000 worth the extra money, how long does each really last, and what similar products are out there. This review answers those questions from Black Jack's own technical data sheets.

We did not test Drive-Maxx ourselves. This review is built from the manufacturer's published data sheets and label directions for each product (published by Gardner-Gibson, the Tampa, Florida company that makes Black Jack), plus the general chemistry of asphalt-emulsion sealers and widely accepted pavement-maintenance practice. Where we could not confirm something, we say so. Read the pail you buy: it is the final word.

⚡ Quick Answer: Yes, Drive-Maxx is a competent consumer sealer range. The number is the rated life in years: 500 = 5 years, 700 = 7 years, 1000 = 10 years. All three need two thin coats at about 350 sq ft per coat (300 on rough asphalt) per 4.75-gallon pail. The 500 and 700 need 55–90°F; the 1000 can go down at 45°F, recoats in 2–4 hours and is ready for light foot traffic in about an hour in good drying conditions. Vehicles wait at least 24 hours on all three.

Is Drive-Maxx Any Good?

For a typical homeowner sealing a residential asphalt driveway, yes. Drive-Maxx products are latex-enhanced asphalt emulsions with sand to fill small cracks — the same basic category as the other well-known consumer sealers on the shelf, such as Latex-ite. Their directions are detailed and consistent across the range, the fast recoat times let you finish a two-coat job in a single day, and the top-tier Drive-Maxx 1000 adds a urethane-enhanced, UV-protected film and a cold-weather application window that few consumer sealers offer.

The catches are the same ones that apply to every product in this category. A two-coat requirement means fewer finished square feet per pail than the coverage figure suggests at first glance. The year rating is a manufacturer claim under ideal conditions, not a resealing schedule. And no sealer fixes structural damage — if your driveway has alligator cracking, potholes or a failing base, those need repair first. Within those limits, Drive-Maxx is a sound choice, and the question is really which tier.

What Drive-Maxx Is

Drive-Maxx is Black Jack's line of blacktop "filler and sealer" products. All of them are water-based asphalt emulsions: fine droplets of asphalt suspended in water with clay and fillers. Black Jack adds latex polymers to the range to improve durability and flexibility — the 500 is described as "latex enhanced," the 700 as "enhanced with latex," and the 1000 as enhanced with a latex polymer and urethane. Each contains sand particles to help fill small cracks and give a skid-resistant finish. They are brown in the pail and dry black, clean up with soap and water while wet, and are not coal tar products (see coal tar vs asphalt emulsion for why that matters).

The range has more than three tiers. Black Jack's data sheets also include a Drive-Maxx 300, described as a "good quality" E-Z stir filler and sealer with latex added, and the brand sells a basic 2-year filler-sealer. Above them, the 500 is "better quality," the 700 "better quality" with a heavy-duty sand mix and gel, and the 1000 "best quality." Most shoppers compare the 500, 700 and 1000, so that is where we focus.

Black Jack also makes the products its sealer sheets tell you to use first: Speed-Fill Elastic Crack Filler for cracks, Speed-Patch for cracks and holes larger than 1 inch, and a permanent pothole patch that needs six weeks to cure before sealing. Our guide to how long driveway crack filler takes to dry covers those waits.

500 vs 700 vs 1000: The Differences

The three products share far more than they differ. All come in 4.75-gallon pails weighing about 49 lbs, all list the same coverage per coat, all require two thin coats applied perpendicular to each other, all need a 36-hour window free of rain, all ask for a slightly damp (not wet) surface at application, and all say they are not recommended or warranted on slopes steeper than 20 degrees. Here is what changes.

Rated life

The number in the name tracks the rated life printed on the pail: 5 years for the 500, 7 for the 700, and 10 for the 1000. Treat these as rankings of durability within the range, not as promises. Thin-film asphalt sealers wear fastest in tire paths and in strong sun, and most homeowners reseal consumer products every two to four years whatever the label says. See how long driveway sealer lasts.

Formula

The 500 is an E-Z stir, latex-enhanced formula with a skid-resistant, rubberized finish. The 700 moves to an "advanced gel" that Black Jack says spreads faster and more easily, a heavy-duty sand mix for added traction, and a durable rubberized finish. The 1000 keeps the gel, adds urethane to the latex polymer ("delivers longest life," per the sheet), and adds UV protection so the surface "stays black longer."

Temperature window

This is the most important practical difference. The 500 and 700 need 55°F and rising but below 90°F, and the sheets say not to apply if overnight temperatures below 50°F are expected within 36 hours. The 1000 uses what Black Jack calls ClimaSeal technology and can be applied from 45°F to 99°F, with the overnight limit lowered to 45°F. That extra ten degrees opens up a lot of spring and fall weekends in northern states. Read what temperature to seal a driveway for why surface and overnight temperatures matter as much as the afternoon high.

Speed

The 500 and 700 take about 3–6 hours before the second coat, 2–4 hours to foot traffic, and 24–48 hours to vehicle traffic. The 1000 cuts the recoat to about 2–4 hours and foot traffic to 1–2 hours — Black Jack says one hour in good drying conditions, which it defines as above 75°F, clear sunny skies and humidity below 35% — and says vehicles need a minimum of 24 hours. Humid or cloudy weather lengthens all of these.

How Many Pails You Need

Because Drive-Maxx requires two coats, the finished coverage of one 4.75-gallon pail is about half the per-coat figure: roughly 175 square feet on a smooth driveway (350 ÷ 2) and 150 square feet on a rough or worn one (300 ÷ 2). Black Jack notes that coverage varies with the condition of the surface, and porous asphalt that has never been sealed will absorb more of the first coat.

How to Apply Drive-Maxx

Black Jack's application directions are the same across the 500, 700 and 1000 except for temperatures and timings. In order:

  1. Check the weather. 55°F and rising but under 90°F for the 500 and 700 (45–99°F for the 1000), and no rain or cold nights for 36 hours.
  2. Turn the pails upside down the night before so settled solids loosen.
  3. Repair first. Fill cracks with Speed-Fill, use Speed-Patch for anything over 1 inch, and let repairs cure according to their own directions. A permanent pothole patch needs six weeks.
  4. Clean and degrease. Pressure-wash the driveway. Scrub oil stains with a degreaser or dish soap so the sealer can bond. Rinse.
  5. Remove puddles and apply to a slightly damp, not wet, surface.
  6. Pour about a third of the pail and spread it with a squeegee — Black Jack says a squeegee must be used to get the correct application rate. A brush can follow if you want texture.
  7. Work the whole driveway in one continuous application to avoid color shading, keeping the coat thin. Thick coats cause tracking and drying problems.
  8. Apply the second coat perpendicular to the first once the first has cured (about 3–6 hours, or 2–4 for the 1000).
  9. Keep off it: foot traffic after 2–4 hours (1–2 for the 1000), vehicles after 24–48 hours.

Common Problems and How to Avoid Them

Tracking and soft spots. Almost always caused by thick coats, cool or humid weather, or driving on it too soon. Two thin coats, the right temperature, and the full vehicle wait solve most of it. See how long driveway sealer takes to dry.

Wash-off. Rain inside the 36-hour window can streak or strip a fresh coat. If it happens, read rain after sealing a driveway.

Peeling. Usually poor prep — dirt, oil, or sealing over a surface that was too wet or still had puddles — or too many coats built up over years. See driveway sealer peeling.

Color shading. Stopping halfway, mixing pails from different batches without blending, or letting one section cure faster than another. Finish in one session and blend pails.

Slick or uneven finish on slopes. Black Jack does not warrant Drive-Maxx on slopes over 20 degrees. On steep driveways, work across the slope and keep coats especially thin.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Drive-Maxx 500, 700 and 1000?

The number is the rated life: 5, 7 and 10 years. The 500 is a latex-enhanced E-Z stir formula; the 700 adds a gel and a heavy-duty sand mix; the 1000 adds urethane, UV protection, a 45–99°F application window (vs 55–90°F) and faster recoat and foot-traffic times. All three need two coats at the same coverage.

How long does Drive-Maxx take to dry?

For the 500 and 700: about 3–6 hours between coats, 2–4 hours to foot traffic and 24–48 hours to vehicles. For the 1000: about 2–4 hours between coats, 1–2 hours to light foot traffic (one hour above 75°F, sunny, under 35% humidity) and at least 24 hours to vehicles. Humid or cloudy weather lengthens all of these.

How long does Drive-Maxx last in the can?

Black Jack's data sheets say to keep the product from freezing and to keep containers closed, but the sheets we read do not print a shelf life. A sealed pail stored indoors above freezing will usually remain usable for a season or more; if the contents have frozen, separated into hard lumps that will not remix, or skinned over heavily, don't use it.

Is Drive-Maxx 1000 worth the extra money?

It is if you need the 45°F window, want to finish a two-coat job in a day, or have a sunny, exposed driveway where the UV protection matters. For a warm-weather job on a driveway you reseal every few years, the 500 or 700 is enough.

What products are similar to Drive-Maxx 500?

Other water-based, polymer-modified asphalt-emulsion filler-sealers in the same price tier, such as Latex-ite Airport Grade or Optimum. Compare them on coverage per pail, number of coats and minimum temperature rather than the year rating. See Drive-Maxx vs Latex-ite.

Can I apply only one coat of Drive-Maxx?

Black Jack's sheets say two thin coats are required. One coat may look fine at first but will be thinner than the product is designed for and is unlikely to reach its rated life.
