Latex-ite sells several asphalt driveway sealers, and two of them sit next to each other on most shelves with similar pails, similar descriptions and a noticeable price gap: Ultra Shield and Airport Grade (sold today as Airport Grade+). People searching "latexite ultrashield vs airport grade" want to know whether the more expensive pail is actually better, whether a "10-year" sealer really lasts twice as long as a "5-year" one, and which to use in cold, hot or snowy climates. This guide answers those questions from Latex-ite's own technical data sheets.

We did not test these sealers ourselves. Everything below comes from the manufacturer's published data sheets (Dalton Enterprises, the Cheshire, Connecticut company behind Latex-ite), label directions, and the general chemistry of asphalt-emulsion sealers. Data sheets get revised; where the revisions disagree, we tell you, and the label on the pail you buy takes priority. If you want the wider Latex-ite line, including the Optimum, start with our main Latex-ite driveway sealer reviews.

⚡ Quick Answer: Buy Ultra Shield if you are sealing in cool weather (its 2019 sheet allows 40°F and rising vs 55°F for Airport Grade), want the longest warranty Latex-ite offers (10 years on the sheet), or want the gel formula and UV protection. Buy Airport Grade if it is warm, the driveway is in decent shape and you reseal every few years anyway — it covers the same 300–500 sq ft per pail and cures in the same 24–48 hours for less money.

The Short Answer

Ultra Shield is the premium product and Airport Grade is the mid-tier product. On paper they share the same base — a water-based asphalt emulsion modified with polymers — the same coverage, the same cure time and the same rain-free window. What you pay extra for with Ultra Shield is a more flexible polymer system, a gel consistency that is easier to spread, a UV-protection claim, a cold-weather cure technology Latex-ite calls ThermaSEAL, and a longer warranty.

Whether those extras are worth it depends almost entirely on two things: the weather window you have, and how long you actually expect a coat to last. If you are sealing on a warm July weekend and plan to reseal in three years whatever the label says, Airport Grade does the job. If your only free weekend is in October, or you want the most durable film Latex-ite makes, Ultra Shield is the one.

What the Two Have in Common

It helps to start with what does not change between the pails, because it is more than most people expect.

  • Chemistry. Both are asphalt-emulsion sealers — fine droplets of asphalt suspended in water — with polymer additives for flexibility. Neither is a coal tar sealer, and both list maximum VOC content under 25 g/L. Both clean up with soap and water while wet. See coal tar vs asphalt emulsion if you are weighing other chemistries.
  • Pail and weight. Both come in 4.75-gallon pails weighing roughly 54–58 lbs depending on the sheet revision. Both are thick brown liquids that dry black.
  • Coverage. Both data sheets give the same figures: 400–500 square feet per pail on smooth and previously sealed driveways, 300–400 square feet per pail on rough and unsealed surfaces, and less on rough road-grade asphalt with large aggregate.
  • Coats. Both recommend thin coats, warn that thick coats cause uneven coverage, tracking and curing problems, and allow a second coat 24 hours after the first.
  • Cure time. Both list a cure time of 24–48 hours at 70°F and 50% relative humidity. Cooler or more humid weather lengthens it; our guide to how long driveway sealer takes to dry explains why.
  • Rain. Both say to apply only when rain is not likely for 36 hours.
  • Applicator. Both say to use a squeegee (Latex-ite sells an 18-inch Seal Right squeegee), and both sheets state that a roller is the wrong applicator and voids the warranty.
  • Prep. Both call for the same steps: clean with Latex-ite Cleaner/Degreaser, scrub oil and gas spots, prime oil stains with Latex-ite Oil Spot Primer (allow half an hour to dry), let the surface dry completely (24 hours recommended), and wait at least one full season before sealing newly paved asphalt.

That list matters because it means switching from Airport Grade to Ultra Shield does not change how many pails you buy, how you apply it, or how long the driveway is closed. The decision is purely about performance, weather and price.

The Real Differences

1. Cold-weather application

This is the biggest practical difference. Airport Grade's data sheet calls for ground and air temperature of 55°F and rising, and its precautions say to avoid applying when temperatures drop below 55°F. Ultra Shield's 2019 data sheet lists application at 40°F and rising, credited to ThermaSEAL technology that it says cures in temperatures down to 40°F. An earlier Ultra Shield sheet listed 55°F and rising, so not every pail on every shelf may carry the 40°F claim — check the label before you plan a cool-weather job around it.

Even with ThermaSEAL, Latex-ite's own directions caution against applying very late in the day, and say that early-spring or late-fall application when the sun is at a low angle can prevent proper curing and cause tracking. A 40°F rating extends your season; it does not make a cold, cloudy, damp afternoon a good time to seal. Our guide to what temperature to seal a driveway goes through the surface-temperature and overnight-low checks.

2. Polymer system and flexibility

Airport Grade is described as "polymer fortified for strength and flexibility." Ultra Shield is described as using an "Ultra Flex" polymer system (a newer revision also mentions "Nano-Guard technology") designed so the sealer adheres better both to the driveway and to itself. In practical terms, more flexible films are better at riding out the expansion and contraction of asphalt through hot days and cold nights without cracking at joints and edges. We have no independent lab data comparing the two films, so treat this as the manufacturer's positioning rather than a measured difference.

3. Consistency and application

Ultra Shield is sold as an EZ-Gel (or "Premium Gel") formula, which Latex-ite says saves time and effort when spreading. Airport Grade is an "EZ Stir" formula. Both are thick liquids; a gel holds on the squeegee and spreads more evenly with less effort, which helps first-time DIYers keep the film thin and even.

4. UV protection

Ultra Shield lists UV protection with the claim that it "stays black and won't fade or dull." Airport Grade's sheet makes no UV claim. All asphalt emulsions gray out over time in strong sun; a UV-resistant formula should slow that, which matters most on south-facing, unshaded driveways.

5. Crack filling

Airport Grade's sheet specifically says it fills hairline cracks. Ultra Shield is sold as a "Driveway Filler Sealer" too. Neither is a substitute for a crack filler on anything wider than a hairline: Latex-ite's own sheets say to let crack fillers cure 24 hours, or until firm, before sealing over them. See best asphalt crack filler and how long crack filler takes to dry.

6. Warranty and price

Ultra Shield's sheets list a 10-year warranty. The Airport Grade sheet we found (revised 2016) lists a 4-year warranty. The current product is sold as Airport Grade+, and many shoppers and some retailers describe it as a 5-year sealer; we could not confirm the current figure from a Latex-ite data sheet, so check the pail. Ultra Shield costs more per pail — how much more varies by store and season.

10-Year vs 4- or 5-Year: What the Ratings Mean

The most common question behind this search is some version of "does Ultra Shield really last 10 years, and Airport Grade only 5?" The honest answer is that neither number is a prediction of when you will need to reseal.

A year rating on a sealer pail is a limited warranty or durability claim made under ideal conditions: proper cleaning and oil-spot priming, a sound, crack-filled surface, thin coats applied with a squeegee, warm dry weather and no rain for 36 hours. Warranties also typically cover the product, not the labor of reapplying it, and depend on following the directions — Latex-ite's own sheets say a roller voids the warranty. Read the warranty terms printed on your pail if you expect to rely on it.

Real-world life depends on traffic, sun, climate, and how thick the film is. Thin-film asphalt emulsions wear first in tire paths and turning areas, and most homeowners find they want to reseal mid-tier consumer products every two to four years regardless of the rating. The pavement-maintenance industry generally recommends resealing when the surface turns gray and the aggregate starts to show rather than on a fixed calendar; see how often to seal a driveway and how long driveway sealer lasts.

So the rating is best read as a ranking within the brand: Latex-ite is telling you Ultra Shield is the more durable formula. Whether it is "twice as durable" is not something the labels prove. If your plan is to reseal every three years anyway, the extra warranty years matter less than the cold-weather window and the gel formula.

Which One for Your Driveway?

Sealing in October or April. Ultra Shield, provided your pail lists the 40°F minimum. Airport Grade's 55°F requirement rules out many fall and spring weekends in northern states, and the 36-hour rain-free window needs to be met too.

Hot climate, unshaded driveway. Ultra Shield's UV-protection claim makes it the better fit for a south-facing driveway in strong sun. In very hot weather, both sheets tell you to mist the pavement lightly before sealing, leaving no puddles, and to avoid applying late in the day.

Snowy, freeze-thaw climate. The more flexible film is the one to choose where pavement moves a lot through the seasons, which points to Ultra Shield. But no sealer survives poor timing: in cold climates, plan the job for a warm stretch well before the first frost so the film can cure fully, and keep steel plow blades off the surface. Latex-ite does not publish freeze-thaw test data we could verify.

Steep driveway. Neither Latex-ite sheet we read gives a slope limit, but any sealer is harder to keep thin and even on a steep grade, and thick puddled areas at the bottom will cure slowly and track. A gel formula like Ultra Shield's is easier to control on a slope. Work across the slope, keep coats thin, and add a little sand only if the label permits it.

Rental, or a driveway due for resurfacing. Airport Grade. If the asphalt will be replaced or overlaid in a few years, there is no point paying for a 10-year rating.

First time sealing a driveway that has never been sealed. Either; plan for the lower coverage figure (300–400 sq ft per pail) and two coats, because porous, unsealed asphalt absorbs much of the first coat. Ultra Shield's gel formula is a little more forgiving for beginners. Read how to seal an asphalt driveway before you start.

Comparing with Black Jack. If your store also stocks Black Jack, the closest match to Ultra Shield is Drive-Maxx 1000 and the closest to Airport Grade is Drive-Maxx 500 or 700. The big difference is that Drive-Maxx requires two coats every time. See Drive-Maxx vs Latex-ite.

Applying Either One Properly

Because the two products share the same directions, the application steps are the same. Latex-ite's sheets lay them out clearly, and getting them right matters more than which pail you buy.

  1. Check the forecast. 55°F and rising for Airport Grade (40°F for Ultra Shield where the pail says so), no rain likely for 36 hours, and start early in the day.
  2. Flip the pails at least 24 hours before, checking the lids are secure first, so settled solids redistribute.
  3. Clean and degrease. Sweep, scrub oil and gas spots with cleaner/degreaser, rinse, and prime remaining oil stains with oil spot primer; let it dry half an hour.
  4. Fill cracks wider than hairline and let the filler cure (Latex-ite says 24 hours, or until firm).
  5. Let the driveway dry completely — 24 hours is recommended. Mist lightly only if the pavement is very hot, leaving no puddles.
  6. "Box" the pails: mix them back and forth between buckets so the color and consistency are uniform. Keep mixed pails covered.
  7. Edge first with a large brush, protecting concrete with a tarp.
  8. Pour and pull. Pour a ribbon of sealer across the driveway about 2 feet from the garage, then drag the squeegee back and forth in a moon shape, working toward the street. Keep a wet edge, apply pressure for a thin coat, and do not push the squeegee.
  9. Finish in one session to avoid different cure rates leaving different shades.
  10. Second coat, if wanted, 24 hours later. Then keep cars off until the sealer has cured — 24–48 hours at 70°F and 50% humidity, longer in cool or humid weather.

If you are not sure whether you need one coat or two, see how many coats of driveway sealer; if rain arrives early, see rain after sealing a driveway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Latex-ite Ultra Shield better than Airport Grade?

Ultra Shield is Latex-ite's top-of-the-line sealer and carries a longer warranty (10 years on its data sheet), a more flexible polymer system, a gel formula, a UV-protection claim and — on its 2019 sheet — a 40°F minimum application temperature versus 55°F for Airport Grade. Coverage, cure time and rain window are the same. It is the better product; whether it is worth the extra money depends on your weather window and how often you reseal.

What is the difference between Latex-ite 10-year and 5-year sealers?

The year figure is the manufacturer's warranty or durability rating under ideal conditions. Ultra Shield carries the 10-year rating. Airport Grade's 2016 sheet lists 4 years; the current Airport Grade+ is often described as 5-year, so check the pail. Neither number predicts when you will need to reseal — most driveways need it every two to four years depending on traffic and sun.

Can I use Airport Grade as a second coat over Ultra Shield, or vice versa?

They are the same type of product from the same manufacturer, so they are chemically compatible. For a single job, though, it is better to use one product for both coats so the finish and cure are uniform and the warranty conditions are clearly met. For a future reseal, either can go over the other once the old coat is clean and sound.

Does Airport Grade hold up in hot, cold and snowy conditions?

It is designed for residential asphalt in normal US climates, but it needs 55°F and rising to apply and has no UV-protection claim. In harsh sun or freeze-thaw climates, Ultra Shield's more flexible polymers and UV protection make it the better fit. In any climate, the timing of the application matters more than the product.

How much area does one pail cover?

Both data sheets give 400–500 square feet per 4.75-gallon pail on smooth or previously sealed driveways, and 300–400 on rough or unsealed surfaces. Divide by those numbers and multiply by the number of coats.

Where does Latex-ite Optimum fit?

Optimum is Latex-ite's mainstream polymer-modified filler-sealer. We cover it alongside Airport Grade in our Latex-ite driveway sealer reviews.
